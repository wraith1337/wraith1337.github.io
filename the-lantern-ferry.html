The Lantern Ferry

Every night, the little wraith rows the lantern route across the mist. Tonight the lake gives something back: a fallen star to carry home, past the sleeping castle, to the guardian who waits where the mist ends. Sixty seconds, drawn and sounded from scratch.

Painted, then breathed

Every scene began as a full ink-and-watercolor plate: the night sky, the moonlit water, the far mountains and castle, the reeds, the pier, the fox guardian, and the little wraith in its boat. Those plates were then animated frame by frame into 3,600 individual 1280 × 720 frames at 60 frames per second - continuous camera drifts, parallax shores, living water with reflections, drifting mist, fireflies, a shooting star, and a paddle that dips with every stroke. Nothing is a browser trick or a handful of drawings slid around with code; the film is assembled from thousands of rendered frames, and the motion never repeats.

The sound is original too: the music-box melody, the paddle strokes and water laps, the crickets, the distant owl, the star's shimmer, and the dawn birds were all synthesized note by note for this film.
